About Me

I am Tadeo Esquivel, a biologist and researcher specializing in the ecology, taxonomy, and conservation of tropical biodiversity, with a focus on continental mollusks. Raised in Cancún, witnessing the rapid environmental transformation of my hometown driven by urban growth motivated me to move to Mexico City to pursue my scientific training at UNAM. My academic path is defined by a strong global perspective—having completed exchange programs in Spain (University of Oviedo) and Germany (Freie Universität Berlin)—and a drive to investigate understudied, highly vulnerable species. I am passionate about integrating rigorous fieldwork, modern ecological modeling, and community engagement to advance biodiversity conservation.

Taxonomic Inventory of Continental Mollusks in Kabah Ecological Park, Quintana Roo

Urban Protected Natural Area | Continental Malacology

Kabah Ecological Park is a crucial remnant of medium sub-evergreen forest embedded within the urban landscape of Cancún, Quintana Roo. This project represents the first systematic and comprehensive assessment of continental gastropod diversity in the protected area, documenting Neotropical malacofauna resilient to habitat fragmentation.

Project Gallery & Scientific Evidence

Figure 2. Shell morphology of the invasive freshwater apple snail Marisa cornuarietis (Ampullariidae) in dorsal, apertural, and ventral views (scale bar = 1 cm).

Figure 3. Forest canopy and urban interface within Kabah Ecological Park, Cancún.

Figure 4. Dumped topsoil mounds inside the park serving as introduction vectors for non-native species.

Figure 5. High-resolution specimen photograph showing extended soft tissue and operculum morphology.

Figure 6. Extreme size comparison: minute land microgastropod positioned on a 50-centavo coin.

Key Findings

  • Taxonomic Richness: Documented 46 continental gastropod taxa, establishing Kabah Park as the site with the highest reported snail richness in Quintana Roo.
  • Geographic Contributions: 46 new taxonomic records for Benito Juárez municipality and 16 new state records for Quintana Roo.
  • Detection of Exotic Species: Recorded the establishment of invasive non-native species including Marisa cornuarietis and the predatory snail Tomostele musaecola.

Methodology & Impact

Field sampling integrated direct manual collection, leaf litter and soil sieving, and stereomicroscopic sorting. All voucher specimens were cataloged and deposited in the National Collection of Mollusks (CNMO) at the Institute of Biology, UNAM.

Invasive Non-Native Species: Ecology, Distribution, and Risk Assessment of Introduced Snail Species in Mexico

Invasive Non-Native Species | Spatial Ecology & Conservation Biology

Biological invasions represent one of the primary drivers of global biodiversity loss. This project evaluates national occurrence dynamics, transport pathways, and ecological risks of introduced and invasive non-native gastropod species across aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems in Mexico. The research integrates national macro-scale spatial analysis (GBIF dataset and Kernel Density Estimation modeling) with specific local case studies on high-risk invasive taxa.

National Mapping & Taxonomic Evidence Gallery

Figure 1. Occurrence of exotic freshwater mollusk species in Mexico (GBIF 2024 dataset). Occurrence density is modeled using Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) contours, contrasting human observations against preserved museum specimens.

Figure 2. Stereomicroscopic shell plate of the invasive predator land snail Tomostele musaecola (Streptaxidae) in apertural, apical, and umbilical views (scale bar = 1 mm).

Figure 3. Shell morphology of the exotic freshwater apple snail Marisa cornuarietis (Ampullariidae) in dorsal, apertural, and ventral views (scale bar = 1 cm).

Figure 4. Live specimen of the invasive Asian clam Corbicula sp. (Cyrenidae) in freshwater habitat.

Figure 5. Uncontrolled dumping of bulk topsoil and ornamental plants in urban forest patches serving as main dispersal vectors for non-native microgastropods.

Research Highlights & Major Invasiveness Studies

  • National Spatial Density Analysis: Processing GBIF occurrence records (human observations and preserved museum specimens) applying Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) contours to identify major introduction clusters and high-risk river basins in central and southern Mexico.
  • First Record of Tomostele musaecola: First documented detection in Mexico of the carnivorous predator snail Tomostele musaecola (Streptaxidae) in Cancún, Quintana Roo, posing a direct threat to native Neotropical microgastropods.
  • Freshwater Bivalve & Gastropod Invasions: Assessment of establishment, ecological impacts, and dispersal pathways of non-native freshwater mollusks such as the Asian clam Corbicula sp. and the apple snail Marisa cornuarietis in Neotropical ecosystems.
  • Anthropogenic Vectors: Identification of commercial landscaping, bulk topsoil transport, and aquarium trade as the leading pathways driving mollusk introductions across Mexico.

Scientific & Policy Impact

These spatial models and taxonomic case studies generate essential baseline data for environmental authorities to design early-detection protocols, biosecurity measures, and management strategies in protected natural areas.

Terrestrial Snail Communities in the Understory and Lower Canopy of Los Tuxtlas, Veracruz

Community Ecology | M.Sc. Thesis at UNAM

Research conducted at Los Tuxtlas Biosphere Reserve in Veracruz, evaluating land gastropod community structure across understory leaf litter and lower canopy microhabitats formed by the understory palm Astrocaryum mexicanum, advised by Dr. Edna Naranjo García.

Fieldwork Gallery & Tropical Rainforest Research

Figure 1. Specialized field sampling in lower canopy microhabitats formed by Astrocaryum mexicanum palms using field ladders.

Figure 2. Characteristic spiny trunk morphology of the understory palm Astrocaryum mexicanum.

Figure 3. Macro photograph of a live land gastropod specimen recorded during leaf litter sampling.

Figure 4. Translucent land snail active on understory foliage inside the biosphere reserve.

Figure 5. Systematic ground quadrat sampling and formal leaf-litter processing with local field assistants.

Figure 6. Neotropical rainforest canopy architecture and buttress root systems in Los Tuxtlas Reserve.

Key Findings

  • Species Diversity: Identified 15 land gastropod species inhabiting understory litter and suspended litter traps inside palm crowns.
  • Taxonomic Novelty: First regional record of the genus Microconus for Los Tuxtlas.
  • Ecological Modeling (pSEM): Utilized piecewise Structural Equation Modeling, demonstrating that A. mexicanum palms indirectly facilitate snail abundance and richness by functioning as suspended litter traps in the lower canopy.

Limnological & Zooplankton Assessment at Zimapán Hydroelectric Reservoir

University Social Service | Institute of Marine Sciences and Limnology (UNAM)

Interdisciplinary community research assessing limnological dynamics at the "Fernando Hiriart Balderas" (Zimapán) Hydroelectric Dam, situated between Hidalgo and Querétaro, evaluating trophic state impacts on local artisanal fisheries.

Limnological Fieldwork & Aquatic Assessment Gallery

Activities & Social Impact

  • Zooplankton Analysis: Processed and identified over 20,000 zooplankton specimens (Rotifera, Cladocera, Copepoda) to class, order, and family levels.
  • Water Quality Diagnosis: Evaluated physicochemical parameters to diagnose reservoir hypertrophication and aquatic food web structure.
  • Community Support: Delivered scientific data to support local fishing cooperatives in sustainable fisheries management.

Award Recognition

For its community impact and scientific rigor, this project was awarded the prestigious "Gustavo Baz Prada 2024" Award by the National Autonomous University of Mexico.

International Collaborations, Conservation Biology & Competitive Grants

Biomechanics, Mohamed bin Zayed Conservation Fund & IUCN Red List Assessment

Collaborative research projects joining international institutions and funding bodies to evaluate functional morphology, fund field conservation, and assess extinction risks for Neotropical continental malacofauna:

1. Freshwater Snails of Sierra Gorda Biosphere Reserve (MBZ Conservation Fund)

International conservation research project co-directed alongside Ph.D. candidate Rodrigo Rangel, funded by The Mohamed bin Zayed Species Conservation Fund (Grant No. 242534065, $6,350 USD). The study evaluates species diversity, microhabitat preferences, and conservation priorities for endemic aquatic gastropods in karst spring complexes across the Sierra Gorda Reserve.

Figure 1. In situ macro photograph of an active freshwater snail specimen grazing on submerged decaying leaf litter in karst spring habitats of Sierra Gorda (MBZ Fund Project, co-directed with Rodrigo Rangel).

2. Functional Biomechanics & Soft Tissue Retraction in Olividae (Senckenberg)

Research collaboration with Dr. Winfried Peters at the Senckenberg Research Institute and Natural History Museum (Frankfurt, Germany), examining soft tissue retraction mechanisms and foot biomechanics in marine gastropods of the family Olividae.

Video 1. In vivo experimental recording showing foot and mantle retraction mechanisms in live Olividae specimens (scale reference: 50-centavo coin). Research collaboration with Dr. Winfried Peters (Senckenberg Research Institute).

3. IUCN SSC Terrestrial & Freshwater Snail Specialist Group

Serving as technical reviewer and expert evaluator for the IUCN SSC Terrestrial & Freshwater Snail Specialist Group, conducting extinction risk assessments and assigning threat categories for Mesoamerican continental mollusks during regional expert workshops.

Figure 2. Regional Mesoamerican expert panel meeting in Guatemala for the IUCN SSC Terrestrial & Freshwater Snail Red List Assessment.
